Dead end, explained honestly

A VIN cannot find your speeding ticket

A speeding ticket is issued to a person — the driver. A VIN, a title, and a registration identify a vehicle. No official court lookup we have found lets you look up moving violations by VIN, because courts file cases by person and case number, not by car.

What each identifier can find

You haveWhat it finds
Citation numberThe case, on the court's official site for that county.
Driver's license numberTickets issued to that driver — only on portals that explicitly offer DL search. For example, Nebraska's JUSTICE searches by name, not DL number — other states differ.
NameCases filed under that name — only where the portal supports name search (for example, Nebraska's JUSTICE portal supports name search, $17 per search — other states differ).
Plate numberCamera, parking, or toll violations in some cities. Not speeding tickets written to a driver.
VINVehicle history: accidents, title brands, recalls. Not moving violations.
Title / registrationOwnership and liens. Not tickets. A title inquiry is not a ticket search.
Beware of sites that promise "VIN ticket history." There is no official source behind that claim. At best they are selling vehicle-history data; at worst they are harvesting your identifiers. Use the official court and DMV pages linked from this site instead.

What to do instead

  • Lost the paper?

    Start a check — pick the county where the stop happened. You can also find the courthouse on the map.

  • Want your full history?

    Request your own DMV driving record — $15 in Nebraska, shows convictions and points.
